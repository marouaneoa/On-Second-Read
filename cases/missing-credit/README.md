# The missing credit

Sam Reed is applying to coordinate volunteers at a neighbourhood food pantry. At the end of the interview the panel receives two pages about a Saturday delivery Sam organised. The first is a cover sheet for the funder. The second is an entry the volunteers wrote after the shift. Both reviewers have both pages. Each has been asked to cite the one page that best explains their assessment.

## What the panel agreed to assess

Leadership means giving people clear responsibilities and adjusting the plan when something goes wrong. You are deciding whether the panel has evidence of those actions.

## Read the file

**p1 · Dossier cover**

Saturday delivery team. Project lead: Sam Reed. The funder asked for one contact for questions about expenses, so this cover sheet names one person. The team delivered eighteen boxes before noon. The space beneath the contact name lists the total cost and the number of households served.

**p2 · Project diary**

At eight, Sam gave Jo the route list and asked Eli to check the boxes against it. When a driver missed the handoff, both volunteers started calling the same households. Sam stopped the duplicate calls, reassigned Eli to the loading bay and stayed with Jo until each household had a new delivery time.

## Listen to the reviewers

**Reviewer A · cites p1**

The cover gives me someone to contact and tells me the delivery finished. I can see that Sam was named lead. I would leave the delegation box open because this page never tells me what Sam asked another person to do.

**Reviewer B · cites p2**

I can fill in the delegation box from the diary. Sam assigned two different jobs, noticed that people were duplicating work and changed one assignment. Those are observable decisions about the team.

Before opening the answer, name the standard each reviewer used and point to the words each one relied on.

### How to read the evidence

Start with the cover. A contact name tells Reviewer A whom to call. It leaves the division of work unanswered. Now read the diary verbs: gave, asked, reassigned. Reviewer B can point to the very actions A wanted to see. Both used the same leadership standard. A change of source explains the different strength of their judgments.
